# 3D, cameras and simulations

This page describes 3D meshes (`Surface` and its shape family), point
clouds, textures, cameras, vector fields, isolines, OBJ models, 3D axes and
the `janim.ode` simulation module. Examples that use them live in
`examples/` (for instance, `examples/scenes/space_scene.py`).

## Meshes: `Surface` and its shape family

`Surface(uv_func, u_range=, v_range=, resolution=(101, 101), color=GREY_C,
opacity=1.0, shading=(0.3, 0.2, 0.4), epsilon=1e-3, normal_nudge=1e-3,
depth_test=True)` samples `uv_func(u, v)` on the grid (`u` outermost, `v`
innermost, as in ManimGL's `surface.py`), stores vertices in `points` and
vertices nudged along the normal in `d_normal_points`; the shader recovers
the normal from the difference. `get_unit_normals()` returns it.
The indices define two triangles per cell (`triangle_indices(nu, nv)`, zero-based).

Every method called ON a mesh operates in double precision (`c + factor *
(p - c)` rounded once) and moves both arrays; a method on a `Group`
containing meshes dispatches to each child with the group's pivot, so
`Cube().rotate(…)` and `Cube()[0].rotate(…)` give the same results as
applying the method to each face. `set_points` requires the same number of
points and moves the normals along with them; `set_color`/`set_opacity`
write per-vertex RGBA; `set_shading(reflectiveness=, gloss=, shadow=)`
preserves omitted values (on a `VMobject`, by contrast, ManimGL's signature
sets omitted values to zero). `interpolate` and `become` require matching resolutions.

Shape family: `Sphere` (exact radial normals), `Torus`, `PlaneXY`,
`Cylinder`, `Cone`, `Dot3D`, `Line3D`, `Arrow3D`, `Square3D`, `Cube` (a
`Group` of six `Square3D` objects; rejects `fill_color`/`stroke_*`), `Prism`,
`Prismify`, `SurfaceMesh`; and the vector solids `VGroup3D`, `VCube`,
`VPrism`, `Polyhedron(vertices, faces)` (face indices START AT 0),
`Tetrahedron`, `Octahedron`, `Icosahedron`, `Dodecahedron`.

## Clouds: `DotCloud`, `TrueDot`, `GlowDots`, `PGroup`

`DotCloud(points, color=GREY_C, opacity=1.0, radius=0.05, glow_factor=0.0,
anti_alias_width=2.0)` stores `points`, `radii` and `rgba` per point. Methods:
`set_points` (resizes radii and colors: truncates or repeats the last),
`set_radius`, `set_radii`, `get_radii`, `get_radius` (the maximum),
`scale_radii`, `set_glow_factor`, `set_color`, `set_colors(colors | float32
rgb(a) rows, opacity=)`, `set_opacity`, `make_3d(reflectiveness=0.5,
gloss=0.1, shadow=0.2)` (shading + z-buffer), `scale(f, about_point=,
scale_radii=True)` (radii scale by `|f|`), `rotate`, `shift`,
`move_to`. The bounding box expands by the maximum radius, including inside
a group. `get_points()` is the LIVE array: an updater can write to it
(this is how a Lorenz simulation with a million particles runs). `GlowDots`
is the glow preset (`radius=0.2`, `glow_factor=2.0`); `PGroup` only
accepts clouds.

## Textures: `TexturedSurface(surface, image, opacity=1.0)`

Copies the mesh and replaces its color with a texture sampled in `uv` (`u`
from 0 to 1, `v` from 1 to 0 by row). `image` is a path or a
`(height, width, 3|4)` uint8 array (`pixels` is shared between copies).
`shift`, `rotate`, `scale`, `set_opacity`, `set_shading`, `interpolate`
(pixels switch at α = 0.5) and `become` behave as on `Surface`.

## Camera

`Camera` provides `get_theta/phi/gamma`, `increment_euler_angles(dtheta=,
dphi=, dgamma=)` (φ is clamped to `[0, π]`), `increment_theta/phi/gamma(value,
units=)`, `scale_frame(factor, about=)` and `reset_camera()`; `CameraFrame`
has them too. Quaternions and Euler angles use Janim's deterministic libm
(see below), so `reorient(30, 70)` gives the same orientation on every machine.

A layer window (`Layer(…, camera=, window=)`) reads `is_fixed_in_frame` from
ANY mobject with that field: a fixed-in-frame `PlaneXY` can serve as a screen.

## Fields: `VectorField`, `TimeVaryingVectorField`, `StreamLines`, `AnimatedStreamLines`

These follow ManimGL's `vector_field.py`: eight points per arrow (two segments
plus the separator anchor), widths `[1,1,1,1,4,2,0,0]`, `tanh` for length,
and the 3b1b colormap (`colormap_3b1b`) applied to magnitude. Streamlines
are integrated with RK4 (`ode_solution_points(f, state0, time, dt=)`) from
a grid with deterministic jitter (`hash01`, splitmix64 over
`(jitter_seed, counter, axis)`) and smoothed with `set_points_smoothly`.
`AnimatedStreamLines` moves a Gaussian width window along each line with
offsets `-lag_range * hash01(phase_seed, i)`. Supported coordinate systems
are `Axes`, `NumberPlane` and `ThreeDAxes`.

## Isolines: `ImplicitFunction(f, x_range=, y_range=, min_depth=5, max_quads=1500, use_smoothing=False)`

Traces the level curve of `f`: a quadtree refined where the sign changes,
dual-point triangulation and traversal of the links. `f` receives Python
floats: division by zero raises `ZeroDivisionError`; write
`np.float64(1) / (x * y)` if the function has poles.

## Models: `ThreeDModel(path, height=3.0, color=WHITE, opacity=1.0)`

Reads OBJ/MTL (`v`, `vt`, `vn`, `f` with negative indices, `mtllib`, `usemtl`,
`Kd`, `map_Kd`). Each `usemtl` section is a part: `TexturedSurface` if the
material has a readable `map_Kd`, otherwise a `Surface` colored by `Kd`
(or `color`). Corners without a normal receive the area-weighted normal.
The result is a `Group` scaled to `height` and centered.

## Axes: `ThreeDAxes`

Numbers are placed AFTER raising the axes; those on `y` and `z` go to
the LEFT (`line_to_number_direction=LEFT`) and the `x` axis keeps its zero
(`skip_zero=False`).

## Simulations: `janim.ode`

`janim.ode` provides a differential-equation integrator with the SciML API:
`ODEProblem(f, u0, tspan)` with in-place `f(du, u, p, t)`, `Tsit5()`,
`init(prob, Tsit5(), reltol=, abstol=)` → an integrator with live `u`,
`step(integ, dt, True)` (advances exactly to `t + dt`) and `solve(prob,
Tsit5(), reltol=, abstol=, saveat=)` with Tsit5's dense interpolant. This is
a step-by-step implementation of OrdinaryDiffEq's Tsit5 method: Hairer's
initial step, a PI controller with `fastpower`, FSAL and tstops. A double
pendulum is chaotic, so the integrator always uses the same arithmetic and
produces identical trajectories on every run.

`janim.ode.dual` provides forward-mode automatic differentiation (in the
style of ForwardDiff): `Dual` (scalar or row-wise), `abs2` (on floats it
returns `Squares`, which remembers the base so `dual_sum` can accumulate
with fused operations as in `sum(abs2, ::Matrix{Float64})`), `dual_sum` and
`gradient(f, x)` in chunks determined by `pickchunksize` (10 for 50).

## Janim's libm

`janim.math` reproduces `sin`, `cos`, `tan`, `acos`, `tanh`, `exp`,
`pow`, `log`, `log10` and `hypot` bit for bit, ported from Julia's standard
library (MIT), plus `fma`, `atan`, `atan2` and the `linear_range` and
`step_range` ranges. Python's `math.sin` depends on each system's glibc and
`np.sin` on each CPU's SIMD path, and both differ in the last digits for
3% of inputs; this is why mesh geometry, cameras and fields use this libm:
the same frames on every machine, and the same as Janim.jl. Each function
accepts a float or an entire array: the array version
(`janim/core/_vector_math.py`) produces the same bits as the scalar version
(`janim/core/_julia_math.py`) and is about five times faster per element,
with an exact emulation of the `fma` that numpy lacks. Three places where
Julia's compiled code does NOT fuse the source's `muladd`
(`cody_waite_2c_pio2`, all of `pow_body`, `_hypot`) are documented in the
scalar module.

## Costs

Building a `Sphere(101, 51)` takes about 10 ms and a `Torus(101, 101)`
about 17 ms: built-in surfaces sample the entire mesh with three array
calls (`Surface(…, vectorized=True)`), and deterministic trigonometry is
vectorized. A `Surface` with a custom `uv_func` samples vertex by vertex
(85 ms for 5,151 vertices) unless the function accepts arrays and
`vectorized=True` is requested.

With many small meshes (hundreds of cubes, thousands of rotating points),
cost is linear in the number of objects; the expensive part is the constant:
`rotate` takes about 85 µs per face versus 1.5 µs for `shift`, because
child-by-child dispatch recalculates each mesh's matrix. A `VMobject` whose
points change takes about 0.6 ms to draw, roughly 70 times its cost when
stationary, because its joins are recalculated member by member.

## A surface's bounding box after writing its points

`Surface.points` is an ordinary array and a scene can write to it in place
(`m.points[:] = …`, `m.d_normal_points[:] = …`). A `Surface`, a
`TexturedSurface` and a `DotCloud` measure their bounding box on every
query: the next `get_center()` sees the new geometry automatically.

The containing `Group` remains cached: if the mesh modified in place lives
inside a group and the query is on the GROUP, notify it with
`m.note_changed_data()`. It does not recalculate automatically because that
cost grows with the number of members (noticeably in a scene with almost
two thousand meshes), and the notification takes one line. A `VMobject`
always caches its bounding box.
`examples/scenes/space_scene.py` and
`examples/tutorial/c2_01_updaters_and_custom_mobjects/01_alpha_updaters.py`
show the pattern.
